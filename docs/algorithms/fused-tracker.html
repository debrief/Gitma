Fused tracker (the IMM with a learned range measurement)

Source: fusedPredict in packages/classical/src/fused.ts, and immRangeUpdate in packages/classical/src/imm.ts. CLI: tma-fuse-track.

Requirements: SRD Annex C §C.11 (owner decision 31), spec 014.

Why​

The phase-4b tracker hybrid passed G5, but it took T2's range and, on most rows, the IMM's velocity. That has two costs:

  • Not one track state. The range and velocity it reports come from different estimators.
  • Worse before a manoeuvre. There it is 1.47 × the IMM's range error, because it always takes T2's range.

The fused tracker keeps one filter and gives it T2's range as information.

Equations​

The frozen reference IMM (CV + coordinated turn on (x, y, vx, vy, ω); trackers) runs unchanged. After each bearing update at sample i, when (i − (zig_index + 1)) is a multiple of M and T2's row at i is converged, each model takes a log-range update:

  • z = ln r̂, where r̂ is T2's range at sample i;
  • h(x) = ln ρ, with ρ² = (x − x_own)² + (y − y_own)²;
  • H = [dx/ρ², dy/ρ², 0, 0, 0];
  • R = (k · σ̂)², where σ̂ is T2's stated log-range sd (log_range_sd);
  • K = P Hᵀ / (H P Hᵀ + R) and x ← x + K (z − ln ρ);
  • P ← (I − KH) P (I − KH)ᵀ + K R Kᵀ (Joseph form).

Guards (spec 015). A row adds nothing if any of these holds:

  • it is not converged;
  • its range is ≤ 0 or not finite, where ln r̂ is undefined;
  • its sd is absent, ≤ 0 or not finite, where R would be singular or meaningless.

No row of the phase-4c test split meets these conditions, so the guards leave the committed result unchanged (spec 015 R4).

The model probabilities are left to the bearings. The combined estimate and the re-initialisation are the IMM's own (immPredict, through its extra step hook). The rule is causal: sample i reads only T2's row at sample i.

Why k and M​

T2's range errors are correlated from one sample to the next, and with the bearings the IMM already uses. Fusing every sample at T2's stated sd counts the same information many times. Two settings temper this, empirically rather than by modelling the correlation:

  • k inflates T2's stated sd.
  • M thins the updates to one every M samples.

On seed-211 validation, 18 variants were scored (experiments/20261102-fused-research/). The best region is k = 4 with M from 1 to 12, together with k = 8, M = 3, at a smallest G5 margin of 0.056–0.075. The frozen choice is k = 4, M = 6 (one update a minute), in the middle of that region. Thinning and inflating are not interchangeable: no single combination such as M·k² orders the variants.

Limits​

  • One state, two sources of information. The fused state is a single IMM state, so its range and velocity agree with each other. On test, its implied bearing rate is as accurate as the IMM's. But it takes a second estimator's range, and that estimator saw the same bearings; k and M are an empirical correction for that, not a model of it.
  • Before any target manoeuvre it only matches the IMM (range 1.03 × on test, interval [0.99, 1.07]). The gains come after manoeuvres.
  • Uncertainty is not calibrated. Like the reference IMM's, its covariance is too wide on typical rows and has an overconfident tail. On test, the median NEES is 1.10 against the IMM's 0.98, where 3.36 is expected; 94.1% of rows are within the 95% bound, against the IMM's 92.7%. Against the nominal bounds it is no worse than the IMM, but the reference's inflation could mask double counting.
  • Course. On overall course it is worse than the phase-4b hybrid: on test, 1.04 × the hybrid, interval [1.03, 1.06].
  • Against the EKF and batch least squares it is still worse before any target manoeuvre. There its course error is 2.2 × the EKF's (4.09° against 1.88°); over all settled rows it is 1.41 ×.
  • It needs T2's stated sd. A network that states no uncertainty cannot be fused this way.
