Phase 6 re-score: G7 passes, with coverage within tolerance on average but not calibrated

November 7, 2026 · 3 min read

G7 on seed 215 was inconclusive: the fused tracker's 50% range interval covered 48.3%, and its confidence interval straddled the 48% floor. The rules allow one re-score on a larger fresh seed. The owner chose to run it. Decision 34 fixed the details before the data existed:

  • What stayed the same: the tracker, the reference, the frozen models, the multipliers and the rules.
  • What changed: a fresh seed, 216, and a test split of 110,000 scenarios instead of 40,000. That is large enough to resolve a coverage of about 48.3% against the floor.
  • The forecast: about an even chance of passing.

Result: pass​

Coverage on seed 216 (184,667 G5 rows)50%90%
Range (log)48.6% [48.3, 48.9]89.9%
Course50.5%90.3%
Speed49.4%89.9%

All nine gated checks pass. The fused tracker's 90% intervals are narrower than the IMM's: 0.73 × for range, 0.94 × for course and 0.89 × for speed. This was the one re-score, so the verdict is final.

What the pass does and does not show​

Five independent reviewers agreed that the pass is correct. One recomputed every value with code of its own, and another re-ran the gate. All five also agreed on how far the claim goes.

  • Range intervals run about 1.5 points short. At 50%, the two seeds together give about 48.5% [48.3, 48.8]. That is inside the ±2-point tolerance but not centred on 50%. The pass rests on the tolerance. The reference IMM, fitted on the same validation data, lands on 50%. So reusing validation data does not explain the fused tracker's shortfall.

  • The intervals cover within tolerance only on the pooled mix of rows. Range at 50% covers:

    RowsCoverage
    Settled, before any manoeuvre (quiet legs)67%
    Settled, after a manoeuvre39%
    At zig + 5 min61%
    At zig + 20 min40%

    Seed 215 showed the same pattern within a point.

  • Course and speed intervals are often uninformative.

    • 17% of course 90% intervals span the whole circle, and the median course 90% interval is 188° wide. The course width ratio of 0.94 therefore compares two mostly vague intervals.
    • 27% of speed 90% intervals reach below zero.

  • This was a second look. Over both seeds, the chance of a false pass is about 5% at most, not 2.5%. A smaller re-score would probably have been inconclusive again.

  • The pass judges these frozen multipliers. The 0.3-point margin is smaller than the multipliers' own sampling error. So the pass does not show that the recalibration procedure works in general.

Phase 6 in one line​

On average over the G5 rows, the fused tracker's recalibrated intervals cover within the tolerance, with range about 1.5 points short, and they are tighter than the IMM's. Within a regime they do not: the intervals are too wide on quiet legs and too narrow after a manoeuvre, and its course intervals are often vacuous. Conditional calibration, for example a multiplier that depends on time since the last manoeuvre, would be a new design. The owner decides on the follow-on.
