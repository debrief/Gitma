Phase 5: frozen models do not beat a CUSUM at detecting manoeuvres

November 5, 2026 · 3 min read

G5 passed in phases 4b and 4c. SRD Annex C §C.6 therefore lists incremental follow-ons, and the first is manoeuvre detection, judged against a CUSUM on the reference tracker's innovations.

The owner chose two things for this attempt:

  • No training. The detector is built from models that already exist: the frozen recurrent tracker T2, and the reference IMM, which now uses decision 32's guarded initialisation.
  • The test. Detection delay, with every detector's threshold set for the same false-alarm rate: one alarm per 2 h of quiet tracking.

What was compared​

  • The baseline. A CUSUM on the IMM's innovations, tuned on validation from 12 declared settings to favour it.
    • Clipping. The innovations are heavy-tailed: a few huge values otherwise hold an alarm for hours. So both the variance kind and the mean-shift kind are clipped.
    • The winner. The two-sided mean-shift CUSUM, since a turn biases the bearings to one side.
  • The learned detectors. Two candidates, declared in advance:
    • T2's course disagreement with the IMM;
    • T2's own course change over the last minute.

Result: it stopped at validation​

At 0.5 false alarms per hour, on 7,479 course changes in seed-211 validation:

Detected within 5 min
CUSUM (mean shift)28.2%
T2's course change over 1 min19.2%
T2 against the IMM5.9%
  • The learned detector is not carried. It is 9 points behind, with interval [−10.2, −7.7] pp, so it was not taken to a test split, and fresh seed 215 was never generated.
  • The delay check could not be judged. Every detector misses more than half the course changes at this rate, so the median delays are infinite. That rule was the agent's, written before the data; any later attempt should declare a delay statistic defined at its operating point.

Reported, not used: a crossover​

The same detectors were also calibrated at looser rates:

False alarms per hour0.5125
CUSUM28.2%38.0%47.8%62.6%
T2's course change19.2%39.0%58.1%71.7%

From about 1 false alarm per hour, T2's course change catches more manoeuvres in time, though about 20–40 s later among those both catch. Moving the operating point after seeing this would choose the rule from the result, so it stays a reported observation.

What next​

The owner decides. Annex C's other follow-ons remain:

  • calibration of the learned uncertainty;
  • realistic bearing error.

Phase 4b: a hybrid passes G5 — a learned range with a tuned tracker's velocity

November 1, 2026 · 4 min read

Phase 4's recurrent tracker T2 beat a tuned IMM tracker overall but lost on straight legs, so it did not reach the test. The owner chose a follow-on: a hybrid of the two frozen estimators, judged by G5 unchanged. The hybrid takes T2's range always, and T2's course and speed only where the two estimators' courses differ by more than 45°. Elsewhere it takes the IMM's.

It passed, on a fresh 40,000-scenario test split (seed 213), decided by the agent review panel in two rounds:

Iteration 5: a hybrid passes G3; the network adds robustness, not accuracy

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read

G2 left the models usable but about 3 × worse than the classical least-squares solver. G3 asks for parity: within 1.2 × classical on every metric, and a lower gross-error rate.

Iteration 5 trained nothing. It combined the two estimators: classical's estimate, except where classical fails or the network disagrees by more than 50% on range, where the network's is used.

G3 passes for that hybrid on a fresh test set of 20,000 scenarios (seed 207):

  • Parity. Every median and 90th percentile of range, course and speed is within 0.97–1.02 × classical.
  • Gross errors. Five minutes after the zig they fall from 1.19% to 0.57% (−0.62 pp [−0.82, −0.42]).
  • Validation agreed. It forecast 1.25% → 0.55% on seed 205.

Iteration 4: velocity outputs pass G2, still 3 × classical

October 25, 2026 · 4 min read

Iteration 4 changed the models, not the problem. It kept iteration 3's three-leg engagements, made the models output a velocity (vx, vy) instead of a course and a speed, and added the derived features (bearing rate, its validity flag, the bearing-line intercept).

G2 passes. The seed-205 test split was inconclusive at 0.981 kt [0.940, 1.031]. The rules allow one re-score on a larger fresh-seed test set, and on seed 206, with 100,000 scenarios and the same frozen models, every check passes.

  • Speed passes. The instantaneous LSTM reaches 0.939 kt [0.926, 0.951] at zig + 600 s on the σ = 1° slice, against 1 kt. The BiLSTM reaches 0.740 kt.
  • Range and course pass easily: 7.3% and 4.7° for the LSTM, 5.3% and 3.1° for the BiLSTM.
  • Better than iteration 3 on the same rows. Range, course and speed are 16–27% better.
  • Still about 3 × classical. Classical reaches 0.32 kt on the same rows. The LSTM is 2.1 × classical on range, 3.5 × on course and 2.9 × on speed.

Records: experiments/20261025-i4-g2-rescore/ and experiments/20261024-i4-g2/. Figures: iteration-4 results.

Iteration 3: a second zig helps everyone, classical most

October 22, 2026 · 4 min read

Iteration 3 changed the problem, not the models. After the owner's realism check in the viewer, ownship now zigs twice, and both turns cross the line of sight. The 2c models were trained from scratch on the new engagements. Speed improved by almost half, but not enough: the iteration stopped at validation under its pre-registered rule, and the test split was not scored.

  • Speed fails the rule. The instantaneous LSTM reaches 1.10 kt [1.06, 1.16] at zig + 600 s on the σ = 1° slice. The rule needed an interval reaching below 1 kt. The forecast, recorded before training, was 1.1–1.3 kt.
  • The BiLSTM gets closest of any model so far: 1.02 kt [0.98, 1.05]. It is reported, not gated.
  • Range and course pass easily: 8.8% and 6.2° for the LSTM, 7.2% and 4.4° for the BiLSTM.
  • Classical gains more. On the same scenarios it reaches 0.34 kt, so the LSTM is now 3.3 × classical on speed, against 2.4 × in iteration 2c.

Record: experiments/20261021-i3a-val/. Figures: iteration-3 results.
