Phase 3b: two hypotheses fix the course, not the speed

October 30, 2026 · 4 min read

Phase 3 found a network that beats a tuned IMM tracker after a target turns, but that averages "turned" and "not turned" on targets that hold course. Phase 3b gave the network a way out. It outputs two estimates and the probability that the target has turned, and reports the more probable estimate.

It half worked. On the same validation split:

MeasurePhase 3bPhase 3aIMM trackerLimit
After a turn: median range error10.3%10.5%13.6%below the IMM
After a turn: median course error14.5°14.4°24.1°below the IMM
Held course: course error6.1°11.1°5.0°10°
Held course: speed error1.15 kt1.60 kt0.71 kt1 kt

The speed interval, [1.05, 1.25] kt, lies wholly above the limit. The pre-registered rule therefore stopped phase 3b at validation, and its test split was never scored. Under SRD Annex B, this was the last phase-3 attempt.

Records: results and the validation record.

What was tested​

Only the network's output changed. The data, the frozen IMM reference and the gate were all as in phase 3.

  • The network gives a held-course estimate, a turned estimate, and P(turned).
  • The flag's training. The training label is the truth: has the target's course changed yet?
  • The forecast in the decision record, written before training, was held-course course about 6° and speed about 0.95 kt (0.8–1.2), with a 40% chance of reaching the test split.

What happened​

  • Course: fixed. Held-course course error fell from 11.1° to 6.1°, as forecast. It no longer grows with time.
  • After a turn: unchanged. The network still beats the IMM by about a quarter on range and 40% on course.
  • Speed: not fixed. Speed fell from 1.60 to 1.15 kt, inside the forecast band but above the limit.

The flag explains the speed failure:

  • It detects real turns well. The median delay is 70 s, and it misses 0.5%.
  • It also false-alarms. On 29–31% of held-course rows five and ten minutes after the zig, it calls "turned". In this data, those minutes are when targets usually start to turn.
  • A false alarm is costly. The turned estimate is biased slow, so each one costs about 1.8 kt.
  • Even without false alarms, speed sits at the limit. Where the network says "held", its speed error is 1.00 kt, so a perfect flag would leave it there too. The IMM manages 0.71 kt and batch least squares 0.31 kt.

Reading it critically​

  • Switching moves the failure rather than removing it. An averaged output blurs every estimate a little. A switched output is sharp, but sometimes sharply wrong: held-course course P90 rose from 34° to 51°.
  • No threshold tuning. A stricter threshold than 0.5 would cut false alarms but slow detection. Choosing it on this split would make validation a training signal, so it was not done.
  • Two changes at once. The output form and its supervision changed together, as declared, so this result cannot separate them.
  • The finding is consistent. Two different network designs beat a tuned tracker after a turn, and both give back accuracy on targets that hold course. Neither dominates the tracker, and dominating it is what gate G4 asks.

What next​

SRD Annex B §B.5's second branch applies, as it did for phase 3:

  1. publish the negative result, which this post does;
  2. the owner then decides separately whether an end-to-end learned tracker is a new project.

Real or recorded data stay deferred either way.
