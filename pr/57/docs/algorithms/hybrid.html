Hybrid estimator (classical with a network fallback)

Source: packages/classical/src/hybrid.ts (hybridSwitch), CLIs tma-hybrid and tma-g3 (packages/eval). Requirements: SRD §5 (G3), Annex A lever B.2.6, spec 009.

Why a hybrid​

After G2, the models were 2.1–3.5 × the least-squares solver on range, course and speed. They made fewer gross errors early in the track. Spec 009's research looked at how the two could be combined:

  • Starting the solver from the network changes almost nothing. It reproduces classical in all but 0.13% of rows.
  • Classical's gross errors are not convergence failures. They are the least-squares optimum itself lying far from the truth. On seed 205's validation split, the true state had the lower cost in 1 of 2,379 gross rows.
  • The network is still right there. Its range was within 50% of the truth in 91% of those rows.

So the hybrid keeps classical's estimate and uses the network only where the two disagree.

Rule​

At each timestep after the zig, with classical row c and network row n (both causal):

  • Classical failed: use n if c did not converge or is implausible (classical then reports converged = false).
  • Large disagreement: use n if |r_c − r_n| / r_n > 0.5, where 0.5 is SWITCH_RANGE_REL, the same bound as a gross error.
  • Otherwise: use c.

The network is the frozen iteration-4 instantaneous LSTM. The rule has no fitted parameters.

What it claims​

It is a hybrid result, not "a network alone":

  • its accuracy is classical's;
  • its contribution is a lower gross-error rate where least squares lands far from the truth.

G3 (SRD §5) is judged for it against classical by tma-g3, on the σ = 1° slice, with paired bootstrap 95% intervals and FR-015:

  • the median and P90 ratios of range and course error at zig + 300 s, and of speed error at zig + 600 s, against 1.2;
  • the gross-error-rate difference at zig + 300 s, against 0.
