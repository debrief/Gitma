Phase 11: retraining T2 on realistic bearing error does not improve its range

November 14, 2026 · 3 min read

Phases 7–10 showed that under realistic bearing error (correlated noise plus a drifting bias), the learned network T2 gives the most robust range estimate. T2 had been trained only on white noise. The obvious next question: does training it on the realistic error make it better still?

The test​

  • The model. T2's architecture and configuration, unchanged, trained from scratch on a fresh 100,000-scenario dataset generated entirely with realistic error. Training took about 16 hours on GitHub Actions and stopped early at epoch 48.
  • The gate (G8). The retrained T2 must:
    • have a median range error wholly below the frozen T2's;
    • pass G5 against the reference IMM.
  • The validation step first. The six point estimates on the shared validation split must all be within their limits before any test data are generated.

Result: no gain on range, so it stops at validation​

  • Range is unchanged. 12.4% against the frozen T2's 12.2%, and the interval also rules out a gain of more than 1%.
  • Settled course improves by about 12%, mostly on straight legs before any manoeuvre.
  • It still beats the IMM on overall range and course, and passes G5's settled limits.

The cost: worse on white noise​

On the same scenarios with white noise only, the retrained T2 is clearly worse:

The retrained T2 looks more robust (it degrades 1.21 × against 1.52 ×), but only because it starts worse. On white noise it loses to the IMM on range.

Baselines (realistic error, all gated rows)​

  • Batch least squares: 17.1% and 46.0°. It is best before any manoeuvre (6.9%, 2.0°).
  • EKF: 16.1% and 29.7°. It is best on settled course (9.8°) and speed (1.91 kt).
  • Naive: 38.7% and 88.3°.
  • Fused tracker and hybrid: rebuilt on the retrained T2, they are unchanged (within 0.3 points in range).

What it means​

Seeing the error in training did not buy range accuracy. A plausible reading is that the frozen T2 is already near what the bearings allow under this error model: phase 9 found the drifting bias nearly unobservable, and a network cannot learn what the data do not contain.

Limits:

  • one training run, so the run-to-run spread is unknown;
  • one error model at one severity;
  • validation data only;
  • an architecture that was not re-tuned.

No test data were generated. Phase 10's verdict stands, and the owner decides what comes next.
