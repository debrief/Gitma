Results: phases 4–11 (end-to-end trackers, synthesis)

Question (SRD Annex C). Can a learned tracker, or a learned component inside a classical one, beat a re-tuned classical tracker on manoeuvring targets? Phases 7–11 also asked whether that holds under realistic, correlated bearing error.

This page closes that line of work. It adds no new data and no new scoring. Every figure below comes from a committed experiment.

What each phase found​

Every gate below was scored once on a fresh test seed, with a pre-registered decision and an agent review panel. "Stopped" means the pre-registered validation rule failed, so no test data were generated.

Detailed write-ups are in the blog, from phase 4 (2026-11-01) to phase 11 (2026-11-14).

What the evidence supports​

1. A learned range estimate is the one consistent gain​

Every pass on range traces to T2's range estimate, whether reported directly (4b, 10) or fused into the IMM (4c, 7). The range ratios against the IMM run from 0.69 to 0.86. On its own, T2 never passed G5 (phase 4). Neither the learned-gain filter (T1) nor the learned detector (phase 5) was competitive.

2. Learning wins around manoeuvres, and classical methods win on straight legs​

  • On straight legs. Before any target manoeuvre, batch least squares is the best estimator in phases 7, 10 and 11: 6.9–7.3% range and 2.0–2.2° course on settled rows, under realistic error.
  • Against the EKF. Every carried estimator loses to the EKF on settled course: the hybrid by 1.44 × (4b), the fused tracker by 1.41 × (4c) and 1.27 × (7), the phase-10 hybrid by 1.25 ×.
  • The gates did not catch this. G5 compares against the IMM, chosen on validation as the better tracker over all rows. That IMM is weaker than the EKF on straight legs.
  • Where the gain comes from. The learned advantage sits in the minutes after a manoeuvre, where the classical filters lag.

3. Under realistic error, nothing beat T2's own range​

T2 was trained on white noise only, yet it is the most robust estimator under correlated error plus a drifting bias. Its error grows 1.47–1.52 × against the IMM's 1.82 ×.

Three phases tried to beat it on range, and all failed:

PhaseApproachRange against T2
8Fusion re-tuned1.014 ×
9Explicit bias state in the IMM1.37 ×
11T2 retrained on the error1.021 ×
  • A plausible reading. The drifting bias is nearly unobservable from the bearings, since phase 9 found the geometry rotates to absorb it. If so, T2 sits near what this data allows.
  • Not tested. This was never checked against a bound.
  • What retraining did. It traded white-noise accuracy for nothing on the realistic error.

4. Accuracy and a coherent track pull in different directions​

  • The per-output hybrids (4b, 10) post the best numbers but are not tracks. The phase-10 hybrid's position and velocity disagree by a median 18 kt over a minute.
  • The fused tracker keeps one state, with a bearing rate as good as the IMM's. Under realistic error that costs it about 6–10% in range against T2 alone.

5. Uncertainty and detection are weaker results​

  • Uncertainty (G7). The pass is pooled coverage, not calibration. Log-range 50% coverage is 67% before any manoeuvre and 39% after one. 17% of course 90% intervals are vacuous.
  • Detection. The learned detector loses to a classical CUSUM at the pre-registered false-alarm rate.

What it does not show​

  • Real data. Everything is simulated: one generator family, flat earth, a known ownship track.
  • Other error models. Realistic error means one model at one severity (Gauss–Markov 1.0°/30 s plus a drifting bias 0.5°/20 min). Phase 7's severity was itself doubled by the owner after the G0 panel.
  • An adapted reference. The IMM reference was tuned on white noise and frozen. It was never re-tuned for the correlated error.
  • Run-to-run variation. Each trained model is a single training run, so the spread between runs is unknown. That matters most for phase 11, whose margin was 2%.
  • Multiplicity across the programme. Each gate controls its own false-pass rate (about 2.5% per look). But eleven phases chose among many designs, and phases 7–11 all selected on the same seed-217 validation split. Fresh test seeds protect each verdict, not the programme's overall choice of what to test.
  • A consistent estimator to beat. Under G8 it moved from T2 (phases 8–9) to the fused tracker (phase 10), by the owner's choice before validation. Phase 10's pass is therefore mostly a replay of phase 7's finding.

Bottom line​

Within this programme, the useful learned component is a range aid for a classical tracker, not a learned tracker that replaces one.

SituationBest choice found
A single coherent trackThe fused tracker (G5 under white and realistic error, with calibratable stated uncertainty)
Straight legsThe EKF or batch least squares
Range alone, under realistic errorT2's range reported directly

Further gains on range under this error model probably need more information in the data, not another model.
