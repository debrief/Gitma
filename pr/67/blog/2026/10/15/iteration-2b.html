Iteration 2b: speed-weighted loss, G2 failed again

October 15, 2026 · 8 min read

Iteration 2b asks one question: does weighting speed in the loss get the iteration-2 models to the G2 speed threshold? It changes nothing else, and judges G2 on a fresh test split.

Verdict: G2 failed (agent review panel, round 2, no rejections).

  • Range passes for both models: 14.6% [14.1, 15.2] instantaneous and 15.8% [15.2, 16.2] time-late at zig + 300 s on the σ = 1° slice, against 20%.
  • Course passes for both: 8.22° [7.88, 8.54] and 8.66° [8.29, 9.03], against 10°. The time-late course failure of iteration 2 is gone.
  • Speed fails for both, by less: 1.77 kt [1.68, 1.83] and 1.77 kt [1.67, 1.86] at zig + 600 s, against 1 kt. The same weights before 2b, scored on the same scenarios, were at 2.02 kt and 2.31 kt. Classical reaches 0.60 kt.

Experiment of record: experiments/20261015-i2c-g2/. Curves and slices: iteration-2b results.

What changed​

The SRD allows one more round after a stalled G2. Iteration 2 left three leads (research R19): derived features, speed in the loss, and the training budget. The user chose the loss (decisions 15–17 in spec 004):

  • Loss weight. The speed term of the loss is weighted 4× (loss_weight_speed: 4). Speed contributed about 15% of the loss before; now it is about 40%.
  • Continued, not restarted. Every model (the two carried ones and the three controls) starts from its committed phase-B weights, to 220 epochs at most with patience 6. Because the loss changed, tma-train re-scores the best epoch under the new loss before it resumes; otherwise the old best, on the old scale, could never be beaten and the old weights would be kept.
  • A fresh test split. The iteration-2 test split (seed 201) decided the first attempt and is never scored again. The gate is judged on the test split of seed 202, generated with the same count and ranges and pinned by content digest in the training plan. Train and validation are unchanged.

Three tooling checks promised at the end of iteration 2 arrived with 2b and were on for this gate: tma-g2 checks the plan's content digests itself, refuses a phase decision that is not a git ancestor of the carried weights, and checks that every estimator was scored on the same rows, not just the same number of them. The evaluation page describes them.

The carried set was fixed before training: the 2b models, unconditionally. Phase B is scored beside them on the fresh split and reported, never gated.

The time-late model's knife-edge G1 fail from iteration 2 (0.514) is now a clear pass. Its deficit since G1 was a training deficit, not an architectural one: given the same budget it matches the instantaneous model on every check. The "not worse" check has no interval; the two exceedances (ratios 1.055 and 1.056 against 1.05) have paired intervals that contain 1.05, so this fail is not distinguishable from a pass. What the speed weight did, and did not do​

  • Speed moved by 12–23% (2.02 → 1.77 kt; 2.31 → 1.77 kt), and range and course improved with it. Nothing got worse.
  • It is still 1.8 kt against 1 kt, with the whole interval above 1.67 kt. The models are about three times classical, which is at the Cramér–Rao bound on these bearings: the panel recomputed the per-scenario bound on this split (median 1.05 kt for the bearings to zig + 600 s) and classical's error is 0.62 of it at the median, where an efficient estimator gives 0.67.
  • The gain is less scatter as much as less shrinkage. The slope of predicted on true speed rose from 0.58 to 0.65 and the residual spread fell from 2.38 to 2.21 kt; correcting the shrinkage alone would not help (it would give 2.3 kt). Target speeds above ownship's 15 kt map to normalised targets above 1, where the shrinkage is worst; the output scaling was not changed.
  • The gap is where classical is near-perfect. By true range at zig + 600 s (all noise):

Classical's 0.60 kt median is carried by the short ranges, where a few hundred metres of range error fixes speed to a fraction of a knot. The models are 1.4–1.5 kt there, flat across bands they resolve well, and only beyond 20 km do they beat classical on speed.

  • The range advantage beyond 10 km is narrower than iteration 2 suggested. At zig + 300 s the models are ahead at 10–20 km (14.2% against 18.6%, all noise) and at 20–30 km (24.1% against 27.4%), but the gap is carried by classical's non-converged tail (117 of 1,806 scenarios at 10–20 km on the σ = 1° slice, scored as infinite error). On converged scenarios alone the two are level, and at zig + 600 s classical is better in every band.

What the panel found​

Five reviewers again: TMA geometry, estimation and numerics, evaluation statistics, reproducibility, and red team. All five returned approve-with-findings in round 1, and none rejected. Every one of them reproduced the speed medians independently, and none found a path from a pipeline error to the verdict.

One latent major, fixed. When a continuation changes the loss, tma-train re-scores the best weights under the new loss before it resumes. It used that value in memory but never wrote it to the manifest, so a chained job (resuming with its optimizer state) compared new-loss epochs against the old-loss number. Had a continuation's first job ended before any epoch beat the re-scored value, later jobs would have kept the old weights silently. It did not happen in this run (the reviewer checked every job boundary in the CI logs), but it could have. The manifest now records the value a later epoch must beat and the source of a continuation, a chained-job test covers the case, and tma-train --check verifies that a finished continuation's kept weights are explained by its record.

What else the panel established:

  • The fresh split is fresh: per-scenario seeds derive from the dataset seed and index, a seed-201 and seed-202 collision would need an index difference of 10¹⁸, and no test scenario shares a seed or parameter set with any of the 100,000 seed-201 scenarios.
  • The fail margin is 16–20 standard errors. Only 30% of scenarios have a speed error under 1 kt; for the interval merely to touch the threshold the test set would have to be about 28 scenarios.
  • The re-scoring worked as specified: the reviewers recomputed the re-scored losses to five decimals and confirmed each model's best epoch was chosen under the new loss. The shuffled control's weights are unchanged from phase B because no epoch beat its re-scored loss, which is the right outcome for a control.
  • Classical's non-converged scenarios (1.4%) are scored as infinite error and included; its 0.60 kt is not flattered.

Nineteen minor findings were answered or fixed in two rounds, mostly record-keeping: the manifests of the committed 2b models do not say they are continuations (the facts are in the review), gate-deciding seeds are now reserved in the plan checker, and the write-up wording above follows the geometry reviewer's corrections.
