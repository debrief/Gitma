---
title: "Looking back over the whole track: better after turns, still worse on straight legs"
date: 2026-11-24
---

Every tracker we tested since phase 3 worked in real time: it had to decide what the target was
doing using only the bearings so far. An analyst reconstructing an engagement afterwards can use
all of them, including the bearings after each turn. So we trained a network that sees the whole
window, and judged it against the classical tools for the same job: smoothers, and the best
forward trackers.

## What we found

- **After turns, it is the best estimator we have.** Its overall range and course errors are the
  lowest of anything tried. Course error falls by a third against our real-time network, because
  it no longer has to guess whether a turn has happened.
- **On straight legs, it is still well behind.** Where the target holds course, its course error
  is 9°, against 3° for a well-tuned Kalman filter and 2° for batch least squares. The pre-registered
  rule asks it to match or come within 10% of every classical candidate on every check, so it
  stopped at validation.
- **The classical smoothers were not the obstacle.** Tuned for range, they came out weaker than
  the forward filters. The forward filter alone settled the outcome.

## What it means

Looking back over the whole track does not change the project's answer. The network's strength is
range, and course after a manoeuvre; classical methods remain better wherever the target holds
course. The useful design is still the combination: a learned aid inside a classical method.
