---
title: "Phase 4c: one track, a tracker that listens to a network's range"
date: 2026-11-02
---

Phase 4b's hybrid passed G5, but it was two estimators stitched together: T2's range beside the
IMM's velocity. Phase 4c keeps **one filter**. The frozen IMM takes the frozen network T2's range
as an extra measurement, weighted by T2's own stated uncertainty and inflated four-fold, once a
minute. Nothing was trained.

**It passed G5** on a fresh 40,000-scenario test split (seed 214), decided by the agent review
panel:

| Fused tracker / IMM, 97.5% interval | Limit | Result            |
| ----------------------------------- | ----- | ----------------- |
| Range, all gate rows                | < 1   | 0.82 [0.81, 0.84] |
| Course, all gate rows               | < 1   | 0.95 [0.94, 0.96] |
| Range, settled rows                 | < 1.1 | 0.86 [0.84, 0.89] |
| Course, settled rows                | < 1.1 | 0.95 [0.93, 0.97] |
| Speed, settled rows                 | < 1.1 | 0.90 [0.87, 0.92] |

Records: the
[gate record](https://github.com/debrief/Gitma/blob/main/experiments/20261102-p4f-g5/g5.md) and the
[panel review](https://github.com/debrief/Gitma/blob/main/experiments/20261102-p4f-g5/review.md).

## What improved on phase 4b

- **One state.** Range, course and speed come from one filter. Projected forward, its bearing stays
  as close to the truth as the IMM's own (1.02° after 5 min against 1.06°), where the hybrid's
  drifted to 1.49°.
- **Settled rows improved because of estimation, not copying.** Settled range, course and speed are
  all better than the IMM's and the hybrid's. The hybrid's settled velocity was mostly the IMM's.
- **The hybrid's worst case is gone.** Before any target manoeuvre the hybrid's range was 1.43 × the
  IMM's. The fused tracker's is 1.03 × [0.99, 1.07]: parity.

## What it does not show

- **It does not beat the IMM before a target manoeuvres.** On validation it seemed to (0.94 ×), but
  on the fresh seed it only matches it. The gains come after manoeuvres: 0.74 × the IMM's range
  error there.
- **The EKF and batch least squares are still better before any manoeuvre.** Against the EKF it
  would fail settled course (1.41 ×). Overall it beats the EKF by a wide margin (range 0.80 ×,
  course 0.78 ×).
- **The hybrid is still better on overall course** (fused 1.04 × the hybrid).
- **The settings were chosen in-sample**, from 18 variants on validation. The decision record says
  12; the panel found the error after scoring, and it is corrected around the frozen record.
  Only the fresh seed decides, and it did.
- **Its uncertainty is not calibrated.** Like the IMM's, its covariance is too wide on typical rows
  and too narrow in a small tail.
- **It is not a learned tracker.** It is a filter that listens to a frozen network. Phases 4b and 4c
  use the same T2 and IMM, so they are not independent replications. Both show that T2's range helps
  this IMM. Neither shows that a network alone tracks better.

## The long view

After G0 to G5 and three phase-4 attempts:

- **What networks add.** A learned range estimate is genuinely useful to a tuned tracker, mostly
  after the target manoeuvres. Folded into the filter as a measurement, it gives one consistent track
  that beats the tuned IMM on every G5 check.
- **What they do not do.** On clean, straight-leg geometry the classical tools (an EKF, a batch fit)
  remain better.

Real or recorded data are still the untested step.
