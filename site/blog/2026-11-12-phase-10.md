---
title: "Phase 10: T2's range with a filter's velocity passes G8, but adds little that was not known"
date: 2026-11-12
---

Phases 7–9 left one clear lesson. Under realistic bearing error (correlated noise plus a drifting
bias), the learned network T2 gives the most robust range, and no change to the filters closes
the gap. Phase 10 takes the direct route and reports T2's range alongside the fused tracker's
course and speed.

## The test

- **The estimator:**
  - range and position from T2;
  - course and speed from the frozen fused tracker;
  - T2's whole row wherever the fused tracker has no estimate (about 2.5% of rows).

  Nothing is trained or tuned.

- **The gate (G8):** pass G5 against the reference IMM, **and** have a median range error wholly
  below the fused tracker's.
- **Pre-registration.** The rules, the validation run and the decision record were all committed
  before the test data existed.

## Result: G8 passes (seed 224, 40,000 scenarios)

| Hybrid against              | Range                    | Course    | Settled range | Settled course | Settled speed |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------ | --------- | ------------- | -------------- | ------------- |
| The IMM (limit 1 / 1.1)     | **0.689**                | **0.895** | 0.724         | 0.887          | 0.898         |
| The fused tracker (limit 1) | **0.908** [0.893, 0.921] |           |               |                |               |

Every 97.5% interval sits wholly within its limit. Five independent reviewers agreed that the
verdict is right.

## Why this is mostly a replay

The panel was blunt about how much this pass adds:

- **The range is T2's, row for row.** The check against the fused tracker is therefore T2 against
  the fused tracker. Phase 7 already showed that (fused/T2 1.095), and phase 8 showed no tuning
  closes it. Seed 224 replicates that result.
- **The velocity is the fused tracker's,** which already passed G5 in phase 7.
- **What is new** is only that the pair, assembled per output, passes every G5 check at once.
- **About 1 point of each margin comes from the scoring convention.** A row with no estimate counts
  as infinitely wrong, and the hybrid never lacks one. On rows where every estimator converged the
  ratios are 0.700 against the IMM and 0.918 against the fused tracker. Both still pass.

## What it does not show

| Median error, all G5 rows | Range | Course    | Speed       |
| ------------------------- | ----- | --------- | ----------- |
| Hybrid                    | 12.0% | 26.6°     | 2.58 kt     |
| Fused tracker             | 13.2% | 27.1°     | 2.60 kt     |
| T2 alone                  | 12.0% | **25.9°** | 2.85 kt     |
| IMM                       | 17.4% | 29.7°     | 2.87 kt     |
| EKF                       | 16.6% | 30.2°     | **2.41 kt** |
| Batch least squares       | 18.0% | 46.6°     | 2.50 kt     |
| Naive                     | 40.2% | 89.9°     | 3.99 kt     |

- **No gain over T2 on range,** since the range is T2's. T2 alone is also slightly better on overall
  course.
- **Classical filters still win on straight legs.** The EKF is better on settled course (10.7°
  against 13.3°) and speed. Before any manoeuvre, batch least squares is best (7.3%, 2.2°).
- **It is not a track.** The position comes from one estimator and the velocity from another. The
  position change over a minute disagrees with the stated velocity by a median 18 kt (8 kt for the
  fused tracker; 17 kt for T2 alone, whose position track is itself jumpy), and the implied bearing rate is the worst of the four estimators. It is a set of
  per-output estimates, not a state you could extrapolate.
- **No uncertainty** is stated for the combined estimate.
- **Scope:** one error model at one severity, on simulated data.

## The arc, phases 7–10

Under realistic bearing error:

- the learned range estimate is the most robust;
- feeding it into a filter costs some of that robustness;
- tuning the fusion doesn't recover it;
- modelling the bias inside the filter fails, because the bias can't be observed.

Reporting the learned range directly keeps its accuracy, at the cost of a coherent track.
