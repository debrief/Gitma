---
title: "Phase 7: realistic bearing error — the fused tracker still beats the IMM, but T2 alone is more robust"
date: 2026-11-08
---

So far every tracker in this project has seen only white bearing noise. Real bearings also carry
correlated error and a slowly drifting bias. Phase 7 asks one question: **does the fused tracker's
G5 advantage over the reference IMM survive that error, with nothing re-trained or re-tuned?**

## The setup

The owner chose three things:

- **frozen trackers;**
- **an error model** made of a correlated term (sd 1.0°, 30 s) and a drifting bias (sd 0.5°,
  20 min). This setting is double the one first proposed. At σ = 1° the total bearing error goes
  from 1.0° to 1.5°;
- **G5's rules, unchanged.**

The generator adds the error from its own random stream, so each scenario can be regenerated
without it. The two versions have identical geometry and identical white noise, which makes a
paired clean comparison possible.

## Result: G5 passes (seed 218, 40,000 scenarios)

| Fused / IMM    | Ratio (97.5% CI)         | Limit |
| -------------- | ------------------------ | ----- |
| Overall range  | **0.766** [0.755, 0.778] | < 1   |
| Overall course | **0.907** [0.897, 0.918] | < 1   |
| Settled range  | 0.774                    | < 1.1 |
| Settled course | 0.875                    | < 1.1 |
| Settled speed  | 0.907                    | < 1.1 |

Validation on seed 217 had already shown much the same: 0.777 for range and 0.922 for course. Five
independent reviewers agreed the verdict is right, and one reproduced it byte for byte.

## What the pass does not show

The absolute errors are large, so the panel narrowed the claim.

| Median error, all G5 rows | Range     | Course  | Speed      |
| ------------------------- | --------- | ------- | ---------- |
| Fused tracker             | 13.2%     | 27°     | 2.6 kt     |
| IMM (reference)           | 17.3%     | 30°     | 2.9 kt     |
| T2 alone                  | **12.1%** | **26°** | 2.9 kt     |
| EKF                       | 16.8%     | 31°     | **2.4 kt** |
| Batch least squares       | 17.6%     | 47°     | 2.4 kt     |
| Naive                     | 40%       | 90°     | 3.9 kt     |

- **T2 alone beats the fused tracker on range**, by 1.10 × [1.08, 1.11] overall. Its error grows
  least under realistic error (1.47 × its clean value, against the IMM's 1.82 ×). The IMM's range
  ends up biased about 7% short under the drifting bias, while T2 stays nearly unbiased. The fusion
  setting, frozen from clean data, gives T2 too little weight. Fusing keeps one coherent track
  state, but under this error it is not the best range estimator.
- **On straight legs, classical methods still win.** On settled rows the EKF beats the fused
  tracker on course (10.6° against 13.5°) and on speed. Before any manoeuvre, batch least squares
  is best of all: 7.3% range and 2.2° course.
- **The reference was frozen, not adapted.** Neither filter models correlated or bias error. An
  IMM with an inflated measurement noise or a bias state might close the gap; this phase does not
  test that.
- **Every tracker is much worse.** Median range error rises 1.5–1.8 × overall and about 2 × on
  settled rows.
- **Scope.** The result covers one error model at one severity, σ = 1° rows and simulated data.

## The one-line version

Under one setting of realistic bearing error, the frozen fused tracker keeps its G5 advantage over
the frozen IMM. The learned range (T2) is what makes it robust, and on its own T2 is more robust
still. The classical filters remain better on straight legs.
