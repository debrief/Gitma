## The question

G1 ([SRD §5](https://github.com/debrief/Gitma/blob/main/docs/SRD.md#5-baselines-metrics-and-success-gates))
passes if, for **both** model variants, the median relative range error 5 minutes after the
ownship zig is below half of the [naive baseline](/docs/algorithms/naive-baseline)'s, on the same
test scenarios. The naive baseline predicts the training-set mean range, course and speed, so a
model that beats it by 2× has learned something about the geometry, not just the prior.

## What G1 cannot tell us

- The targets move at constant velocity and the ownship zig is instantaneous; nothing here says how
  the models cope with manoeuvres or gradual turns.
- σ = 0.5° and no dropouts only; noise and gaps are iteration 2's question (G2).
- The time-late model's estimate at zig + 300 s uses bearings recorded after that time. It is a
  post-hoc reconstruction, not a real-time solution.
- The interval covers test sampling, not training variation: each model was trained once, and
  weights differ across runner CPU families.
- The shuffled-label test rules out leakage through labels and the training harness; truth hidden
  in the inputs is ruled out by a separate counterfactual test.

The full list, with mitigations, is in
[`specs/002-iteration-1-signal/g1-limits.md`](https://github.com/debrief/Gitma/blob/main/specs/002-iteration-1-signal/g1-limits.md).
