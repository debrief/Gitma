---
title: "The network's range inside an EKF beats the re-tuned EKF (with caveats)"
date: 2026-11-16
---

Re-tuning the classical trackers to realistic bearing error stopped the IMM-based fused tracker:
after a target manoeuvre it held course worse than a plain EKF. So we fed the network's range
estimate into an EKF instead, tuned its process noise together with the fusion weights on
validation data, and judged the one chosen setting once on 40,000 fresh scenarios.

## What we found

- **It passed G5 against the re-tuned EKF.** Range error 12.8% against 16.3% (0.78 ×) and course
  error 28.9° against 35.3° (0.82 ×) over all gate rows; after the target settles, within the
  allowed 10% margin on course (1.04 ×). We had forecast only a 10% chance of a pass.
- **The network is needed, but it is not the whole story.** The same EKF with the higher process
  noise and no network fails the gate. About half the course gain comes from that process noise
  alone, which the reference, tuned for range, never used.
- **Straight legs still belong to the classical methods.** Before any manoeuvre the plain EKF holds
  course to 3.3° and least squares to 2.2°; the fused tracker manages 5.2°. It wins after a
  manoeuvre (22° against 33°). The gate pools both, and the mix decides it.
- **It does not beat everything.** Against the re-tuned IMM, course is level (0.99 ×). The network
  alone still has the lowest overall range (12.0%) and course (26.0°).

## What it means

The project's qualified go gets firmer: a learned range aid inside a classical tracker beats a
classical tracker re-tuned to the same error, on range and overall course. It remains a range aid.
On a long straight leg the network's correlated range error makes the tracker worse than bearings
alone, so a weight that fades once the bearings fix range is the obvious next step.
