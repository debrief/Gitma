---
title: "Phase 8: re-tuning the fusion cannot make it beat T2 on range under realistic error"
date: 2026-11-10
---

Phase 7 found a gap. Under realistic bearing error (correlated noise plus a drifting bias), T2 on
its own had lower range error than the fused tracker that feeds T2's range into the IMM: 12.1%
against 13.2%. The fusion's weighting had been tuned on clean data and then frozen, so the obvious
question was whether re-tuning it closes the gap.

## The test

The owner chose to re-tune only the fusion's two settings, on validation data with realistic error:

- k, how much to trust T2's range;
- M, how often to feed it in.

The gate would require both of these:

- **beat T2 alone** on median range error;
- **still pass G5** against the reference IMM.

The grid had 24 settings. The rule for choosing between them was written and committed before any
of them was scored. A setting went to the gate only if its estimated margin on every check was
positive.

## Result: no setting qualifies

| Setting                                     | Fused / IMM range | Fused / T2 range |
| ------------------------------------------- | ----------------- | ---------------- |
| Frozen (k 4, every 6 samples)               | 0.777             | 1.064            |
| **Best re-tune (k 3, every sample)**        | 0.740             | **1.014**        |
| Heaviest weight on T2 (k 0.5, every sample) | 0.749             | 1.027            |

- **Re-tuning helps against the IMM.** Feeding T2's range at every sample cuts the fused
  tracker's range error further (0.74 × the IMM's), and cuts its shortfall against T2 from 6.4%
  to 1.4%.
- **It never beats T2.** The best setting is still 1.4% worse on range. Trusting T2 more does not
  help either: course and velocity then fall apart (course up to 1.27 × the IMM's on settled
  rows), and range still trails T2.
- **So phase 8 stops at validation.** No fresh test data were generated, and phase 7's verdict
  stands.

## Why, plausibly

Under the drifting bias, the IMM's range ends up about 7% short. Every bearing update pulls the
shared track state back towards that biased range, so a single filter state cannot hold T2's less
biased range, however the two are weighted. This explanation was not tested here.

## What it means

Within this design, fusing T2's range into a filter that assumes white noise costs some range
accuracy under correlated error. The filter's state stays coherent, and it still beats the IMM
clearly.

Two further changes might close the gap:

- a filter that models the bias;
- reporting T2's range directly, as the phase-4b hybrid did.

Either would be a new decision for the owner.
