# Results: iteration 5 (hybrid estimator, G3 passed)

**Question:** can a network help reach [G3](iteration-4.mdx), "within 1.2× classical on every
metric, and lower gross-error rate" (SRD §5)? The models alone are 2.1–3.5 × classical. Spec 009
judged a **hybrid** (Annex A B.2.6, decision 24).

- **The estimator.** Classical's least-squares estimate, replaced by the frozen iteration-4 LSTM's
  where classical fails or the two ranges differ by more than 50%. See the
  [algorithm note](../algorithms/hybrid.md).
- **Training.** None.

:::tip G3 passed, for a hybrid
**All seven checks pass** on seed 207's test split (20,000 scenarios, σ = 1°):

- **Ratios.** Every median and P90 of range, course and speed is within 0.97–1.02 × classical,
  against 1.2.
- **Gross errors, five minutes after the zig.** They fall from 1.19% to 0.57%: −0.62 pp
  [−0.82, −0.42].

**What it is not.** It is not a network matching classical. The LSTM alone is still about 3 ×
classical on speed. The hybrid's accuracy is classical's (its medians are about 1% worse).
What the network adds is robustness where least squares lands far from the truth.

## Where the switch helps and hurts

At zig + 300 s, 140 σ = 1° rows switch (1.4%):

- **Helps (82 rows).** A classical gross error is fixed. These cluster at long range (beyond
  12 km, gross errors fall 95 → 23), at low bearing rate and after shallow ownship turns.
- **Hurts (21 rows).** A good classical row becomes gross. These are mostly at short range (under
  4 km, gross errors rise 2 → 20), where classical is sharp and the network's relative range
  error is large.

At zig + 600 s, which is reported and not gated, the gross rates are 0.17% for the hybrid against
0.14% for classical: 11 against 9 rows, no evidence of a difference.

**At σ = 2°**, also reported and not gated, the benefit is larger: gross errors fall from 4.63% to
1.80% at zig + 300 s.
