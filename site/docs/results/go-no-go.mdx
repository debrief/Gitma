# Go/no-go: the project's answer

**The question (SRD §1).** Can a neural network trained on large volumes of simulated
bearings-only data produce usable target range, course and speed estimates?

:::info The answer
- **No-go for a network as a stand-alone TMA solution.** On straight legs, where classical
  methods are near-optimal, the network is 1.6–3.5 × worse than classical least squares. That holds
  for range, course and especially speed. It never reached classical parity alone.
- **Qualified go for a learned range aid inside a classical tracker.** A fused tracker uses the
  network's range as an extra measurement in a classical IMM. It beats the tuned IMM on range and
  course, under white noise and under realistic correlated error. That is the one result worth
  carrying forward. The qualifications are listed below.
:::

## How the answer was reached

Each step was pre-registered, judged on a fresh test seed, and reviewed by an agent panel. The
detailed pages are under Results.

| Step | Question | Outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| G0 | Is the pipeline correct? | Passed, re-run on every generator change |
| G1 | Is there any signal? | Passed: range error well under half the naive baseline's |
| G2 | Is it useful (range < 20%, course < 10°, speed < 1 kt at σ = 1°)? | Failed three times on speed, then stopped at validation once and was inconclusive once. Passed on iteration 4's re-score, at 0.94 kt |
| G3 | Is it at classical parity? | **Not for the network.** On a straight-leg problem the LSTM is 2.1 × classical on range, 3.5 × on course and 3.0 × on speed (BiLSTM 1.6 / 2.3 / 2.3 ×). G3 passed only for a hybrid that uses classical least squares and falls back to the network when classical fails. That hybrid halves gross errors at zig + 5 min. |
| G4 | Can it beat a tuned tracker after a target turn? | After the turn, yes (range 0.77 ×, course 0.60 ×). But it fails on targets that hold course, by hedging towards a turn, so it stopped at validation twice |
| G5 | Can an end-to-end learned tracker beat a re-tuned IMM? | **Not alone.** It fails settled course (1.44 ×) and speed (1.66 ×). It passed only combined with the IMM, as a hybrid (4b) or fused (4c) |
| G6 | Can it detect manoeuvres better than a CUSUM? | No: 19% against 28% detected within 5 min |
| G7 | Is the uncertainty calibrated? | Pooled coverage passed on a re-score. Conditional calibration was not achieved |
| G5/G8 under realistic error | Does it hold up under correlated error and bias? | The network's range is the most robust estimate. The fused tracker passes against the IMM. Nothing tried improved on the network's own range |

## The pattern

The network is strong exactly where classical methods are weak, and weak where they are strong:

| Network strongest | Classical strongest |
| --- | --- |
| Range, and early range convergence | Speed and course on long straight legs, near the Cramér–Rao bound (classical speed 0.3–0.6 kt against the network's 0.9–1.5 kt) |
| The minutes after a target manoeuvre, while filters lag | Before any manoeuvre (batch least squares: about 7% range and 2° course under realistic error) |
| Gross-error suppression on hard geometries | Coherent, consistent track states |
| Robustness to correlated bearing error and slow bias | |

This is why every pass after G2 combines the network with a classical estimator. It is also why
"usable" depends on what the estimate is for.

## Distance from the information limit (spec 024)

Cramér–Rao bounds computed under the exact bearing-error model (seed 217 validation) sharpen the
answer:

- **Straight legs.** Batch least squares reaches the bound on range, course and speed, with white
  noise and with realistic error. T2 is 1.5 × the bound in range under realistic error and
  2.5 × with white noise. Its robustness to the error is mostly slack, not extra information.
- **After manoeuvres.** Only a genie bound (manoeuvre known) can be computed. T2 is 2.2 × above
  it, the fused tracker 2.5 × and the IMM 3.3 ×. Nothing reaches it, and how close a better
  estimator could get is open.

That strengthens the stand-alone no-go. It also places the open room after manoeuvres, where the
qualified go already looks.

## Sensitivity to bearing-error severity (spec 026)

The realistic error model was scaled ×0 (white noise), ×0.5, ×1 and ×2 on seed 217's validation
split, with every estimator frozen (`experiments/20261115-p13-sweep/results.md`):

| Median range error, all G5 rows | ×0 | ×0.5 | ×1 | ×2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fused tracker / IMM | 0.84 | 0.79 | 0.78 | 0.77 |
| T2 / fused tracker | 1.05 | 0.99 | 0.95 | 0.80 |
| Least squares / bound, straight legs | 1.03 | 1.00 | 1.01 | 1.01 |

- **The fused tracker's gain over the IMM holds at every severity,** but levels off above ×0.5.
- **Least squares stays at the bound on straight legs** at every severity.
- **T2 alone overtakes the fused tracker from about ×0.5,** and by 20% at ×2. The fusion and the
  IMM were tuned on white noise, and the IMM degrades fastest.
- **The composite's fit check breaks at ×2:** correlated error trips it on straight legs.

So the qualified go holds, with two sharper conditions: the fusion and the reference both need
re-tuning to the error level that real data show.

## Re-tuned references (spec 027)

Spec 026 showed the reference IMM was tuned on white noise. Annex E re-tuned the IMM and the EKF
to the realistic error on seed 217's validation split, rebuilt the fused tracker on the re-tuned
IMM over phase 8's 24 fusion settings, and required a setting to beat both
(`experiments/20261115-p14-val/validation.md`):

| Median, G5 rows | Range | Settled course |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Re-tuned EKF | 15.8% | 8.3° |
| Re-tuned IMM | 15.1% | 11.7° |
| Best fused tracker (k 4, M 12) | 12.5% | 10.7° |
| T2 | 12.2% | 17.7° |
| Least squares | 17.3% | |
| Naive | 38.7% | |

The range gain survives (0.79 × the EKF, 0.83 × the IMM), but no setting matches the EKF's course
after a manoeuvre (best 1.29 ×, limit 1.1), so it stopped before the gate. The network fixes range;
the IMM's dynamics still cost course. The gain is a range aid, as the synthesis said, not a better
tracker overall.

## T2's range fused into the EKF (spec 028)

Annex F fused T2's range into the EKF instead, with the EKF's process noise σa tuned with the
fusion weights, and **passed G5 on fresh seed 233** against the re-tuned EKF
(`experiments/20261116-p15-g5/`):

| Seed 233 test, median over G5 rows | Range | Course | Settled course, before any manoeuvre | Settled course, after a manoeuvre |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **EKF-fused** (σa 0.07, k 4, M 3) | **12.8%** | 28.9° | 5.2° | **22.1°** |
| Re-tuned EKF (σa 0.03, the reference) | 16.3% | 35.3° | **3.3°** | 32.9° |
| Re-tuned IMM | 15.8% | 29.2° | 7.8° | 24.2° |
| IMM-fused (Annex E's best) | 12.6% | 27.6° | 7.4° | 21.0° |
| T2 | **12.0%** | **26.0°** | 15.8° | 21.6° |
| Least squares | 17.6% | 46.4° | **2.2°** | 61.0° |
| Naive | 40.0% | 90.3° | 90.8° | 89.8° |

- **The gate:** overall range 0.78 × and course 0.82 × the reference; settled course 1.043
  [1.013, 1.078] against a 1.1 limit, so within the margin but significantly worse.
- **The network is needed.** A plain EKF at σa 0.07 fails the same gate on range (1.03 ×) and
  settled course (1.12 ×) (post hoc, reported). But about half the overall-course gain comes from raising σa, which the
  range-tuned reference never did; the range gain is T2's.
- **Straight legs are still the classical trackers'.** Before any manoeuvre the fused tracker's
  course is 1.57 × the EKF's and 2.4 × least squares'. The pooled settled check passes because
  rows after a manoeuvre, where it is much better, make up 45% of settled rows.
- **Against the re-tuned IMM** (not gated) course is level (0.99 ×); range is 0.81 ×.

## A fading weight and adaptive process noise (spec 029)

Annex G tried to remove the EKF fusion's straight-leg cost: T2's weight fades as the filter's range
firms up, and σa rises with the recent bearing NIS. The gate would have added straight-leg course
(within 1.1 × the reference's) to G5. It **stopped at validation** (best score −0.129,
`experiments/20261118-p16-val/validation.md`):

- Adaptation that fires often recovers overall course (29.8°, still 1.04 × the IMM's) but spends
  straight-leg course (5.4°, EKF 3.1°); firing rarely does the reverse. Under correlated bearing
  error the NIS does not separate manoeuvres from noise well enough.
- The fade moves straight-leg course and range toward the EKF's (3.6°, 8.9%) but gives up the
  network's range overall (13.9% against 12.5%).
- Even near the reference's σa, fusing T2 costs straight-leg course (1.2 ×).

So straight legs remain the classical trackers' (and least squares'); the fused tracker's value is
after manoeuvres and overall, as phase 15 found.

## A trained manoeuvre detector (spec 030)

Three designs failed on telling a manoeuvre from correlated bearing error, so Annex H trained a
detector network for it (T2's architecture, realistic-error data) and judged it at a revised G6
against the best CUSUM re-tuned to the same error. It **stopped at validation**
(`experiments/20261118-p17-val/validation.md`):

| At 0.5 false alarms per quiet hour | Course changes detected within 5 min |
| --- | --- |
| Best re-tuned CUSUM | 12.2% |
| Trained detector | 11.1% |
| T2's own course change (untrained) | 10.7% |

- Under realistic error every detector is weak at a strict false-alarm rate (phase 5 reached 28%
  under white noise).
- Training for the label did not beat T2's untrained course change, and it was slower than the
  CUSUM on the turns both caught.
- From about 2 false alarms per hour the learned signals catch slightly more than the CUSUM.

Retrained to fire in the first 2 minutes of a turn (spec 031, Annex I), the detector became fast
(median delay 70 s, half the CUSUM's) but caught only 5.0% of turns within 5 min, and also stopped
at validation (`experiments/20261119-p18-val/validation.md`).

So manoeuvre detection, the weak point behind the composite solver and adaptive process noise,
is not fixed by a trained detector at a strict operating point, with either label.

## Conditions on the "go"

The fused tracker is the candidate. Before it is used outside this project, each of the following
needs closing:

1. **Real or representative data.** Everything here is simulated: one generator family, flat
   earth, a perfectly known ownship and one realistic-error model. Testing on recorded or
   independently simulated data is outside this public repository (OFFICIAL, mock data only).
2. **A stronger reference.** Re-tuned to the realistic error (Annexes E and F), the fused
   tracker on the IMM stopped at validation, but **T2's range fused into the EKF passed G5 against
   the re-tuned EKF** (seed 233). **Partly met:** it is not shown better than the re-tuned IMM on
   course, part of its course gain is process-noise tuning, and on straight legs the EKF and least
   squares stay better.
3. **The size of the gain.** Range error falls to 0.77–0.82 × the IMM's. That is real but
   moderate, and it comes from the network's range alone.
4. **Run-to-run spread.** Each model is a single training run.
5. **Consistency.** The IMM fusion's stated uncertainty passes pooled coverage only, and its
   covariance is not calibrated (NEES median 1.1, where 3.36 is expected). The EKF fusion's
   covariance was not evaluated; T2's correlated errors (0.97 at one fusion interval, 0.77 at
   300 s) make its k 4, M 3 weighting about 2 × overconfident by zig + 20 min.

## How much to trust this answer

- **What supports it.** Every gate was pre-registered and scored once on a fresh seed, beside naive
  and classical baselines. Verdicts are byte-reproducible from committed weights, and every gate
  had a five-reviewer panel.
- **What weakens it:**
  - **Stopping rules were relaxed mid-project.** G2's stop was amended twice (2c, then Annex A)
    before it passed. Each amendment was recorded before the scoring it affected, but "G2 passed"
    means "passed after three fails, a stop and an inconclusive", not "passed as first planned". G2's speed threshold was
    also moved from 5 to 10 minutes after the zig, on the evidence that classical itself needs
    that long.
  - **Many looks.** Eleven post-G2 phases chose among many designs. Phases 7–11 all selected on
    the same validation split. Fresh test seeds protect each verdict, not the programme's choice
    of what to test.
  - **The reviewers are not fully independent.** The panels were AI agents of the same model
    family as the implementer, reviewing read-only. They found real problems (several majors per
    gate), but they are not an external review.
  - **The owner chose key settings,** including the realistic-error severity (doubled after the G0
    panel) and, in phase 10, the estimator to beat.

None of these turns a no-go into a go. The no-go for a stand-alone network rests on large, repeated
gaps against classical methods (1.6–3.5 ×) that no amendment touched.

## Recommendation

- **Stop** developing learned stand-alone TMA estimators on this simulated problem.
- **If the work continues,** take the fused tracker forward as a range aid to a classical tracker.
  Use representative bearing error, re-tune the reference to it, and run several training runs.
  Start where the network's advantage lives: after manoeuvres, and under correlated error.
