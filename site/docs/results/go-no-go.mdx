# Go/no-go: the project's answer

**The question (SRD §1).** Can a neural network trained on large volumes of simulated
bearings-only data produce usable target range, course and speed estimates?

:::info The answer
- **No-go for a network as a stand-alone TMA solution.** On straight legs, where classical
  methods are near-optimal, the network is 1.6–3.5 × worse than classical least squares. That holds
  for range, course and especially speed. It never reached classical parity alone.
- **Qualified go for a learned range aid inside a classical tracker.** A fused tracker uses the
  network's range as an extra measurement in a classical IMM. It beats the tuned IMM on range and
  course, under white noise and under realistic correlated error. That is the one result worth
  carrying forward. The qualifications are listed below.
:::

## How the answer was reached

Each step was pre-registered, judged on a fresh test seed, and reviewed by an agent panel. The
detailed pages are under Results.

| Step | Question | Outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| G0 | Is the pipeline correct? | Passed, re-run on every generator change |
| G1 | Is there any signal? | Passed: range error well under half the naive baseline's |
| G2 | Is it useful (range < 20%, course < 10°, speed < 1 kt at σ = 1°)? | Failed three times on speed, then stopped at validation once and was inconclusive once. Passed on iteration 4's re-score, at 0.94 kt |
| G3 | Is it at classical parity? | **Not for the network.** On a straight-leg problem the LSTM is 2.1 × classical on range, 3.5 × on course and 3.0 × on speed (BiLSTM 1.6 / 2.3 / 2.3 ×). G3 passed only for a hybrid that uses classical least squares and falls back to the network when classical fails. That hybrid halves gross errors at zig + 5 min. |
| G4 | Can it beat a tuned tracker after a target turn? | After the turn, yes (range 0.77 ×, course 0.60 ×). But it fails on targets that hold course, by hedging towards a turn, so it stopped at validation twice |
| G5 | Can an end-to-end learned tracker beat a re-tuned IMM? | **Not alone.** It fails settled course (1.44 ×) and speed (1.66 ×). It passed only combined with the IMM, as a hybrid (4b) or fused (4c) |
| G6 | Can it detect manoeuvres better than a CUSUM? | No: 19% against 28% detected within 5 min |
| G7 | Is the uncertainty calibrated? | Pooled coverage passed on a re-score. Conditional calibration was not achieved |
| G5/G8 under realistic error | Does it hold up under correlated error and bias? | The network's range is the most robust estimate. The fused tracker passes against the IMM. Nothing tried improved on the network's own range |

## The pattern

The network is strong exactly where classical methods are weak, and weak where they are strong:

| Network strongest | Classical strongest |
| --- | --- |
| Range, and early range convergence | Speed and course on long straight legs, near the Cramér–Rao bound (classical speed 0.3–0.6 kt against the network's 0.9–1.5 kt) |
| The minutes after a target manoeuvre, while filters lag | Before any manoeuvre (batch least squares: about 7% range and 2° course under realistic error) |
| Gross-error suppression on hard geometries | Coherent, consistent track states |
| Robustness to correlated bearing error and slow bias | |

This is why every pass after G2 combines the network with a classical estimator. It is also why
"usable" depends on what the estimate is for.

## Conditions on the "go"

The fused tracker is the candidate. Before it is used outside this project, each of the following
needs closing:

1. **Real or representative data.** Everything here is simulated: one generator family, flat
   earth, a perfectly known ownship and one realistic-error model. Testing on recorded or
   independently simulated data is outside this public repository (OFFICIAL, mock data only).
2. **A stronger reference.** The gated reference IMM was tuned on white noise and is weaker than
   the EKF on straight legs. Against the EKF, the fused tracker loses on settled course by
   1.27–1.41 ×. A fair go needs a reference tuned to the real error.
3. **The size of the gain.** Range error falls to 0.77–0.82 × the IMM's. That is real but
   moderate, and it comes from the network's range alone.
4. **Run-to-run spread.** Each model is a single training run.
5. **Consistency.** The fused tracker's stated uncertainty passes pooled coverage only, and its
   covariance is not calibrated (NEES median 1.1, where 3.36 is expected).

## How much to trust this answer

- **What supports it.** Every gate was pre-registered and scored once on a fresh seed, beside naive
  and classical baselines. Verdicts are byte-reproducible from committed weights, and every gate
  had a five-reviewer panel.
- **What weakens it:**
  - **Stopping rules were relaxed mid-project.** G2's stop was amended twice (2c, then Annex A)
    before it passed. Each amendment was recorded before the scoring it affected, but "G2 passed"
    means "passed after three fails, a stop and an inconclusive", not "passed as first planned". G2's speed threshold was
    also moved from 5 to 10 minutes after the zig, on the evidence that classical itself needs
    that long.
  - **Many looks.** Eleven post-G2 phases chose among many designs. Phases 7–11 all selected on
    the same validation split. Fresh test seeds protect each verdict, not the programme's choice
    of what to test.
  - **The reviewers are not fully independent.** The panels were AI agents of the same model
    family as the implementer, reviewing read-only. They found real problems (several majors per
    gate), but they are not an external review.
  - **The owner chose key settings,** including the realistic-error severity (doubled after the G0
    panel) and, in phase 10, the estimator to beat.

None of these turns a no-go into a go. The no-go for a stand-alone network rests on large, repeated
gaps against classical methods (1.6–3.5 ×) that no amendment touched.

## Recommendation

- **Stop** developing learned stand-alone TMA estimators on this simulated problem.
- **If the work continues,** take the fused tracker forward as a range aid to a classical tracker.
  Use representative bearing error, re-tune the reference to it, and run several training runs.
  Start where the network's advantage lives: after manoeuvres, and under correlated error.
