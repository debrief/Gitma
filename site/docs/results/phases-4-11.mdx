# Results: phases 4–11 (end-to-end trackers, synthesis)

**Question (SRD Annex C).** Can a learned tracker, or a learned component inside a classical
one, beat a re-tuned classical tracker on manoeuvring targets? Phases 7–11 also asked whether
that holds under realistic, correlated bearing error.

This page closes that line of work. It adds no new data and no new scoring. Every figure below
comes from a committed experiment.

## What each phase found

Every gate below was scored once on a fresh test seed, with a pre-registered decision and an agent
review panel. "Stopped" means the pre-registered validation rule failed, so no test data were
generated.

| Phase | What was tried | Outcome | Headline (ratio to the reference IMM unless stated) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 4 | Two trackers trained from scratch: T1 (learned gain) and T2 (recurrent, Gaussian head) | Stopped | T2 range 0.85, but settled course 1.44 and speed 1.66; T1 range 2.18 |
| 4b | Hybrid: T2's range always, T2's velocity on large course disagreement | **G5 pass** (seed 213) | Range 0.862, course 0.904; settled 1.02 / 0.98 / 0.99 |
| 4c | Fused tracker: T2's range as a measurement inside the IMM | **G5 pass** (seed 214) | Range 0.824, course 0.947; settled 0.86 / 0.95 / 0.90 |
| 5 | Manoeuvre detection from T2, against a CUSUM | Stopped | Detects 19.2% of course changes within 5 min, against the CUSUM's 28.2% |
| 6 | Recalibrated stated uncertainty | **G7 pass** on the re-score (seed 216); the first look was inconclusive | All nine coverage checks within ± 2 pp; widths 0.73–0.94 × |
| 7 | Realistic bearing error; trackers frozen | **G5 pass** (seed 218) | Fused range 0.766, course 0.907 |
| 8 | Re-tune the fusion so it beats T2 alone | Stopped | Best fused/T2 range 1.014 |
| 9 | IMM with a bearing-bias state | Stopped | Range 0.998 × the IMM's, 1.37 × T2's |
| 10 | T2's range with the fused tracker's velocity | **G8 pass** (seed 224) | Range 0.689, course 0.895; 0.908 × the fused tracker's range |
| 11 | T2 retrained on realistic error | Stopped | Retrained/frozen T2 range 1.021 [0.991, 1.052]; 1.29 × on white noise |

Detailed write-ups are in the blog, from phase 4 (2026-11-01) to phase 11 (2026-11-14).

## What the evidence supports

### 1. A learned range estimate is the one consistent gain

Every pass on range traces to T2's range estimate, whether reported directly (4b, 10) or fused into
the IMM (4c, 7). The range ratios against the IMM run from 0.69 to 0.86. On its own, T2 never passed
G5 (phase 4). Neither the learned-gain filter (T1) nor the learned detector (phase 5) was competitive.

### 2. Learning wins around manoeuvres, and classical methods win on straight legs

- **On straight legs.** Before any target manoeuvre, batch least squares is the best estimator in
  phases 7, 10 and 11: 6.9–7.3% range and 2.0–2.2° course on settled rows, under realistic error.
- **Against the EKF.** Every carried estimator loses to the EKF on settled course: the hybrid by
  1.44 × (4b), the fused tracker by 1.41 × (4c) and 1.27 × (7), the phase-10 hybrid by 1.25 ×.
- **The gates did not catch this.** G5 compares against the IMM, chosen on validation as the
  better tracker over all rows. That IMM is weaker than the EKF on straight legs.
- **Where the gain comes from.** The learned advantage sits in the minutes after a manoeuvre,
  where the classical filters lag.

### 3. Under realistic error, nothing beat T2's own range

T2 was trained on white noise only, yet it is the most robust estimator under correlated error
plus a drifting bias. Its error grows 1.47–1.52 × against the IMM's 1.82 ×.

Three phases tried to beat it on range, and all failed:

| Phase | Approach | Range against T2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 8 | Fusion re-tuned | 1.014 × |
| 9 | Explicit bias state in the IMM | 1.37 × |
| 11 | T2 retrained on the error | 1.021 × |

- **A plausible reading.** The drifting bias is nearly unobservable from the bearings, since
  phase 9 found the geometry rotates to absorb it. If so, T2 sits near what this data allows.
- **Not tested.** This was never checked against a bound.
- **What retraining did.** It traded white-noise accuracy for nothing on the realistic error.

### 4. Accuracy and a coherent track pull in different directions

- **The per-output hybrids (4b, 10)** post the best numbers but are not tracks. The phase-10
  hybrid's position and velocity disagree by a median 18 kt over a minute.
- **The fused tracker** keeps one state, with a bearing rate as good as the IMM's. Under realistic
  error that costs it about 6–10% in range against T2 alone.

### 5. Uncertainty and detection are weaker results

- **Uncertainty (G7).** The pass is pooled coverage, not calibration. Log-range 50% coverage is
  67% before any manoeuvre and 39% after one. 17% of course 90% intervals are vacuous.
- **Detection.** The learned detector loses to a classical CUSUM at the pre-registered false-alarm
  rate.

## What it does not show

- **Real data.** Everything is simulated: one generator family, flat earth, a known ownship
  track.
- **Other error models.** Realistic error means one model at one severity (Gauss–Markov 1.0°/30 s
  plus a drifting bias 0.5°/20 min). Phase 7's severity was itself doubled by the owner after the
  G0 panel.
- **An adapted reference.** The IMM reference was tuned on white noise and frozen. It was never
  re-tuned for the correlated error.
- **Run-to-run variation.** Each trained model is a single training run, so the spread between
  runs is unknown. That matters most for phase 11, whose margin was 2%.
- **Multiplicity across the programme.** Each gate controls its own false-pass rate (about 2.5% per
  look). But eleven phases chose among many designs, and phases 7–11 all selected on the same
  seed-217 validation split. Fresh test seeds protect each verdict, not the programme's overall
  choice of what to test.
- **A consistent estimator to beat.** Under G8 it moved from T2 (phases 8–9) to the fused tracker
  (phase 10), by the owner's choice before validation. Phase 10's pass is therefore mostly a
  replay of phase 7's finding.

## Bottom line

Within this programme, the useful learned component is a **range aid for a classical tracker**,
not a learned tracker that replaces one.

| Situation | Best choice found |
| --- | --- |
| A single coherent track | The fused tracker (G5 under white and realistic error, with calibratable stated uncertainty) |
| Straight legs | The EKF or batch least squares |
| Range alone, under realistic error | T2's range reported directly |

Further gains on range under this error model probably need more information in the data, not
another model.
