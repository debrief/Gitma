Teaching the detector to fire early: faster, but blind

November 19, 2026 · One min read

Our trained manoeuvre detector had been taught that the whole five minutes after a turn count as "manoeuvring", which rewards a late, lingering alarm. So we retrained it to fire only in the first two minutes of a turn, and judged it exactly as before.

What we found​

  • It became fast. When it fires, it does so about 70 seconds into a turn: half the delay of the classical CUSUM detector on the turns both catch.
  • It became blind. Allowing one false alarm every two hours, it caught only 5% of turns within five minutes, against 12% for the CUSUM and 11% for the earlier version.
  • The two labels bracket the classical detector. One is about as complete but slower, the other faster but far less complete. Neither beats it.

What it means​

Under realistic bearing error, the turns that can be detected at all at a strict false-alarm rate are the ones a well-tuned classical detector already finds. The limit is the error, not how the network was taught. Manoeuvre detection stays the weak point of every design that relied on it.
