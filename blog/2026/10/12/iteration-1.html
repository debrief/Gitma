Iteration 1: TODO(continuation) — is the signal learnable?

October 12, 2026 · 18 min read

Iteration 1 asks the question the whole project exists for: given simulated bearings and the ownship track, can a small neural network estimate target range better than a guess from the prior?

Verdict: TODO(continuation) G1 pass | fail, decided by the agent review panel, round TODO(continuation). Experiment of record: experiments/TODO(continuation)/.

Attempt 1 (experiments/20261011-g1-r2/) did not pass as run. The instantaneous model met the criterion with room to spare (0.419 × naive, 95% interval [0.407, 0.431]). The time-late model missed it (0.551 × naive, [0.538, 0.565]) after its training was cut off by a wall-clock cap at 25 of its 60 epochs. The review panel found the pipeline sound and judged the time-late model undertrained, not signal-free. A single, pre-registered continuation of that model to the same 60-epoch budget is the last chance for G1. Full curves and slices: iteration-1 results.

The question​

G1 (SRD §5) passes if, for both model variants, the median relative range error 5 minutes after the ownship zig is below half of the naive baseline's, on the same test scenarios. The naive baseline predicts the training-set mean range, course and speed, so a model that beats it by 2× has learned something about the geometry, not just the prior.

The two variants answer different questions. The instantaneous model is causal: its estimate at a given time uses only bearings up to that time, as a real-time tracker would. The time-late model sees the whole recorded window, including bearings after the gate point, and so is a post-hoc reconstruction.

Each ratio is judged with a paired bootstrap 95% interval: pass if the whole interval is below 0.5, fail if it is wholly at or above, otherwise inconclusive. Further checks guard the result: a model trained on shuffled labels must score like naive (leakage), each recurrent model must beat a non-recurrent control, and time-late must not be worse than instantaneous. How these combine is on the evaluation page.

What was trained​

Four small models over the same masked 240-row input window (see models), plus one leakage run, trained on GitHub-hosted runners (4 cores, no GPU). Every model had max_epochs 60, patience 3 on validation loss and a 5.5 h wall-clock cap (max_wall_s 19,800; 2 h for the shuffled run). Epochs are counted from 0, so "60 (best 58)" means the 59th epoch gave the kept weights.

Why the time-late model was undertrained​

All models shared the same budget in time, not in epochs: a 5.5 h wall-clock cap per model, inside GitHub's 6 h job limit. The bidirectional LSTM runs the window in both directions and has twice the instantaneous LSTM's parameters (82,948 vs 41,476); one epoch took about 775 s against 276 s. The cap therefore stopped it after 25 epochs while the instantaneous model ran all 60. Its validation loss was still falling (0.592, 0.565, 0.571 over the last three epochs; best at epoch 23).

The panel compared like with like. On the post-zig validation rows (the rows that are scored), the time-late model at epoch 23 had a loss of 0.406; the instantaneous model at the same epoch had 0.478 and needed until epoch 31 to reach 0.405. At equal training, time-late was ahead.

What the panel found​

Five independent agent reviewers (TMA geometry, estimation and numerics, evaluation statistics, reproducibility, red team) reviewed attempt 1 over two rounds; the full record, with every finding and response, is in experiments/20261011-g1-r2/review.md.

In plain words:

  • The pipeline is sound. Padding does not leak into the bidirectional pass (padded and unpadded windows give identical outputs); predictions line up with their scenarios; the causal models ignore bearings after the time they estimate; the time-late model does use later bearings (cutting them off makes it worse, 21.2% → 27.2%).
  • The instantaneous pass is real. No truth reaches the inputs or the normalisation, naive is not artificially weak, every comparison uses the same 9,784 scenarios, and the shuffled-label model trained down to the "know nothing" level and scores like naive.
  • Reproducible. A reviewer regenerated the data and re-scored the committed weights locally: the verdict file came out byte-identical, naive and classical metrics byte-identical, model metrics within 1.3 × 10⁻⁵ relative.
  • The time-late fail is a budget effect (one reviewer rejected in round 1 until this was recognised). Calling it "ML not extracting the signal" would overstate it. The first result is recorded as: G1 not passed as run; the instantaneous variant met the criterion; the time-late variant failed under a 25-epoch wall-clock budget and is plausibly undertrained; no evidence that the time-late signal is absent.
  • But no rule allowed a re-run. Turning "undertrained" into "try again" needed an explicit amendment, decided after seeing the result and written down before any new training. That is what the user decided (below), and round 2 approved the protocol with all five reviewers at approve-with-findings.

Smaller findings, reported rather than fixed now: pre-zig rows, never scored, carry 64% of the time-late model's training loss; model manifests lack a git commit field (provenance is recorded in the review instead); training budgets should be set in epochs, not wall-clock time, because runner CPUs vary; the shuffled model's median course error (82.9°) is a little below chance (≈ 90°), a weak bearing-as-course prior rather than leakage.

The decision and the continuation protocol​

The user decided on 12 October, after the first G1 result and before any further training, to continue the time-late model once, to the same 60-epoch budget the instantaneous model had. SRD §5 was amended to allow exactly this, and the protocol was fixed in review.md before the run started:

  1. One change only: no wall-clock cap. Training resumes from the committed epoch-23 weights (digest checked) and runs to epoch 60 or early stopping (patience 3). Epoch 24 of the first run is dropped.
  2. Unchanged: data, splits, training seed, architecture, loss, learning rate, batch size. The other four models are reused untouched; the instantaneous model is not retrained.
  3. Optimizer: Adam's state was never saved in attempt 1, so it restarts once, at epoch 24, and epochs 24–26 do not count towards patience. From then on Adam's state is saved with every checkpoint and restored if a job is interrupted, so a resumed run matches an uninterrupted one (tested: identical weights).
  4. Scoring: the verdict of record comes from re-scoring all five committed models with evaluate-g1.yml on main; the test split is not touched until training completes.
  5. Outcomes, fixed in advance: both intervals wholly below 0.5 and shuffled labels at naive → G1 pass, even if time-late-not-worse still fails (the record then says "time-late not shown better than instantaneous"). A ratio fail of the continued model is final: no further continuation, seed or architecture change, and the project stops per SRD §5.
  6. Record: attempt 1 (0.551) stays recorded as a valid attempt. Every job of the continuation is listed with its run id, epochs covered and runner CPU.

What G1 cannot tell us​

  • The targets move at constant velocity and the ownship zig is instantaneous; nothing here says how the models cope with manoeuvres or gradual turns.
  • σ = 0.5° and no dropouts only; noise and gaps are iteration 2's question (G2).
  • The time-late model's estimate at zig + 300 s uses bearings recorded after that time. It is a post-hoc reconstruction, not a real-time solution.
  • The interval covers test sampling, not training variation: each model was trained once, and weights differ across runner CPU families.
  • The time-late model gets two training stretches with an optimizer restart between them; the instantaneous model got one. The protocol equalises epochs, not the exact training path.
  • The shuffled-label test rules out leakage through labels and the training harness; truth hidden in the inputs is ruled out by a separate counterfactual test.

The full list, with mitigations, is in specs/002-iteration-1-signal/g1-limits.md.
