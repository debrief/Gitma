## What happened on held course

The network under-reports speed on targets that hold course:
- its median signed speed error is −1.16 kt, and 74% of its estimates are slow;
- the IMM's median signed error is +0.24 kt, with 35% slow.

Its median course error on held-course targets grows over time:

| Time after zig | 2 min | 5 min | 10 min | 15 min |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Network | 6.0° | 11.1° | 11.5° | 11.1° |
| IMM | 6.0° | 5.0° | 3.7° | 4.5° |

In this data a turn usually starts 2–8 min after the zig (R1). A network trained with squared
error on velocity reports the conditional mean when it cannot yet tell whether a turn is
starting. An average over possible headings has a shorter vector and a hedged direction. It buys
its post-turn advantage with held-course accuracy: the tracker's dilemma, moved inside the
network.

## Reading it critically

- **The post-turn result is strong, and it is not the claim that decides.** The network is about
  23% better on range and 40% better on course than the best tuned classical tracker five minutes
  after a turn.
  - It holds in every timing slice, including turns after ownship's last manoeuvre, where the
    network's priors are the only extra information.
  - This is the in-principle advantage Annex B set out to test.
  - G4 also requires no regression on held-course targets, and that requirement fails.
- **The control is the absolute G2 bar, not parity with the tracker.** On held course the network
  is also behind the tuned-turn IMM (11.1° against 5.0°, 1.60 against 0.71 kt) and far behind
  batch least squares (1.2°, 0.31 kt).
- **One look, on validation.** The forecast (decision 26) put P(pass) at about 30%, with range
  the likely failure. Range and course instead passed clearly, and the held-course control
  failed: the forecast did not anticipate the hedging.
- **What would address it** (not run here; any of it is new work under §B.5):
  - an output that can represent "turned or not" (a mixture or multi-hypothesis head);
  - a loss that does not reward averaging;
  - a separate turn-detection output (Annex B §B.5's first follow-on, which presupposed a pass);
  - a hybrid: the network after a detected turn, the tracker or batch least squares otherwise.
