## What the pass does not show (panel round 1)

- **On settled rows before any manoeuvre, the hybrid's range is worse than the IMM's.** Settled rows
  split (`settled-slices.json`):

  | Settled rows | n | Hybrid range | IMM range | EKF range | Hybrid course / speed | IMM course / speed |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | Before any manoeuvre | 16,231 | 6.0% | 4.1% (hybrid 1.47 ×) | 3.7% | 4.0° / 0.70 kt | 4.0° / 0.70 kt |
  | After a manoeuvre | 13,475 | 10.3% | 15.2% (hybrid 0.68 ×) | 16.9% | 17.2° / 2.72 kt | 18.6° / 2.80 kt |

  The pooled settled-range ratio (1.022) averages these two regimes. Rows before any manoeuvre are
  the larger share (55%); the pooled check passes only because the post-manoeuvre rows carry it. The hybrid's range is T2's, and
  T2 loses on clean geometry before a target manoeuvres. That is the commonest case, and there a tuned
  filter is better by about 2 percentage points of range.
- **Against the EKF the hybrid would fail two settled checks:** course 1.44 × and speed 1.21 ×
  (settled range 1.07 ×). It beats the EKF overall (range 0.83 ×, course 0.75 ×). The IMM was frozen
  as the reference by overall median range on validation (decision 29), so the gate is as
  pre-registered; but "better than a tuned tracker on straight legs" is not shown.
- **The settled velocity checks pass largely by construction.** The hybrid uses the IMM's velocity on
  about 84% of all per-timestep rows (782,642 of 4,929,538 take T2's; on validation's G5 rows the
  share taking T2's was 13%), so settled course and speed are close to 1 × the IMM.
- **Batch least squares beats the hybrid on settled range** (6.6% against 7.6%).
- **Not a track state.** Range from one estimator and velocity from another do not satisfy the
  bearing-rate relation of either; propagated forward, the hybrid's state would drift off the
  bearings. G5 scores range, course and speed separately and cannot see this.
- **IMM rows with no converged estimate** (147 rows, 0.22%) take T2's velocity and score the IMM as an
  infinite error, so a small part of the gross-error difference is definitional.
